    If the language of our practice carries values we never agreed to, what can we change without permission?

    A manifesto for digital experience designers entering an industry that speaks differently to the one that trained us.

    We were taught that design is never neutral. Then we entered a profession that treated its own language as if it were.

    is often cast as the human voice within technology: the practice that brings people, context and consequence into systems governed by engineering and business. Yet the field rarely begins on neutral ground. By the time a designer enters a project, the business model may already have defined the desirable behaviour, may already have defined the measurable person, and professional language may already have translated those arrangements into common sense.

    Professional vocabulary carries the authority of the institutions that produce and circulate it. Big Tech does not only produce products. Its companies produce analytics platforms, , content standards, hiring criteria and case-study conventions. Capitalist commercial priorities such as growth, engagement, retention, personalisation and scale therefore travel beyond one organisation as apparently universal measures of good digital experience design. We learned to call people , attention , refusal , agreement , departure and infrastructure . No one presented this vocabulary as an ideology. It arrived as competence.     02 · The infrastructure

    Language is designed infrastructure.

    Practised at every stage of the design process, and long after it.

    It frames the research question, determines whose account counts as evidence, converts organisational ambition into briefs and measures, makes those aims executable through and , and addresses the public through flows, labels and . Beyond the project, it travels into case studies, job ads and the portfolios we are hired from. It is a system for coordinating action that also sets the limits of what a team can recognise, defend and imagine otherwise.

    9. Beyond the process

    Infrastructure is most effective when it withdraws from attention. A term can pass through a meeting precisely because it no longer sounds like a position: “reduce ” appears operational even when the friction is a pause in which someone could understand, reconsider or refuse; “increase ” appears quantitative even when it instructs a team to make departure more difficult. A judgement becomes infrastructural when teams no longer experience it as a judgement. Designers are taught to interrogate the politics of platforms, algorithms, defaults and interfaces. This manifesto turns the same scrutiny on the vocabulary moving through them.

    03 · The clearest case

    How consent became a conversion. How consent became a conversion.

    Consent was fought for: in medical ethics, in and, above all, by feminist movements that insisted a yes only counts where a no was possible, and that it can always be withdrawn. Its ethical and legal force depends on its conditions:

    1. 01InformedYou know what you are agreeing to, and who benefits.
    2. 02SpecificAgreement to this use, not every future use.
    3. 03Freely givenSaying no costs you nothing you were already owed.
    4. 04OngoingIt is asked again as things change, not assumed once.
    5. 05RevocableWithdrawing is as easy as giving.

    kept the word and discarded the conditions. Product discourse recasts consent as a flow, a or a conversion event: on the screen, agreeing takes one click and refusing takes a search. The governing question moves from Could this person meaningfully refuse? to How many people completed acceptance? Name the checkpoint , and the person’s hesitation becomes resistance in the machine.

    The spelling survives. The obligation does not.

    This is how values no one voted for are installed. A fought-for standard of autonomy, won against patriarchal power, becomes a performance indicator in the of a business, and refusal remains visible only as failed completion. Where refusal is not a live option, the accurate word is acceptance, and designers are free to use it.

    04 · Six terms of practice

    Six terms of practice

    Each restores what professional language edits out.

    01

    Restore users the person to the research.

    Research is listening, not harvesting.

    The people we study are not , or a of data.

    We name people by their relationship to what we make, ask how they would describe themselves, and centre those on whom the outcome lands.

    On the landing: user, harvest, capture, frontier, data exhaust, native

    02

    Restore the algorithm the actor to the action.

    A brief records a decision, not a condition.

    Everything is designed. Someone decided.

    We write who decided, who benefits and who bears the cost into every brief, and we do not let or sign on anyone’s behalf.

    On the landing: best practice

    03

    Restore engagement the motive to the metric.

    A metric is a value, not a fact.

    A metric can decide what a person is allowed to want.

    Before designing to move a number such as or , we ask what behaviour it rewards, what experience it cannot register, and whose definition of success it installs.

    On the landing: engagement, conversion, impressions

    04

    Restore the consent rate the refusal to consent.

    Consent is a condition, not a conversion.

    Consent that cannot be refused in one action is not consent. It is paperwork.

    We design the way out with the same care as the way in, and protect the pause in which someone can understand, reconsider or refuse. Where refusal is not live, we report the as what it is.

    On the landing: consent rate, accept all, friction, churn, seamless

    05

    Restore the cloud the labour and the material to the system.

    Maintenance is design.

    Nothing digital is weightless. Someone moderates it, labels it, cools it and repairs it.

    We credit the labour and account for the water, land and energy behind and anything we call .

    On the landing: cloud, serverless, automated

    06

    Restore the copy the consequence to the decision.

    Change the decision, not the sentence.

    If you can’t argue with it, it wasn’t designed. It was issued.

    Changing the word is not the intervention; changing what the word allows the organisation to do is. When accurate wording makes a decision indefensible, we reopen the brief rather than polish the sentence. Where speaking carries risk, we act together: document, annotate, preserve the history.

    It has been done: edge case → stress case (2016) · dark pattern → deceptive pattern · master → main (GitHub, 2020) · blacklist → ruled out (Google).
